## PART 6 — HONEST NOTES BEFORE YOU SUBMIT

**The company is new. The experience is not — lead with that.** The LLC is 2026. The principal
has 32 continuous years behind it, ending with six as a federal construction inspector. Never let
a GC read "founded 2026" without the career next to it: 32 years against a field of brand-new LLCs
is the strongest single line in this application, and it answers the "years in business" question
before it is asked.

**A portfolio is not a resume.** Twelve portfolio images are good marketing for homeowners and
weak evidence for a GC. GCs want dollar values, dates, contract type, and who else was on the job.
Fill in real numbers.

**North Carolina is a per-project threshold, not a per-year one.** N.C.G.S. § 87-1(a) deems you a
general contractor — and therefore requires an NC license — only where "the cost of the undertaking
is forty thousand dollars ($40,000) or more." That figure was raised from $30,000 by House Bill
488 effective **October 1, 2023**, so anything under $40,000 may be bid and built in NC with no
NC license at all. § 87-14 likewise only requires proof of licensure (or of an exemption) before
being entitled to a permit on work at or above that amount.

What this means in practice:

- **Under $40,000 in NC** — bid it, pull the permit, no NC license needed. Most repair, drywall,
  roofing, flooring, bath and small kitchen work in Currituck County and Elizabeth City falls here.
- **At or over $40,000 in NC** — an NC license is required before you are entitled to the permit,
  not merely before you invoice. A whole-home gut rehab, an addition, or a multifamily conversion
  will cross it routinely. Either get licensed or decline that scope.
- The threshold is measured **per project**, so several sub-$40k jobs in a year are fine; one
  $40k job is not.
- `§ 87-10(a1)` also tiers NC licences once you do hold one — Limited to $750,000, Intermediate to
  $1,500,000, Unlimited above that — which is a separate axis from the $40,000 threshold.

`[VERIFY — ELECTRICAL, HIGH CONFIDENCE. A standing bilateral reciprocity agreement exists between
the NC State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors and the Virginia Board for Contractors.
VA Class A Contracting + Master Tradesman Electrical maps to ANY NC electrical classification —
Special Restricted, Limited, Intermediate and Unlimited. VA Class B maps to SP/Limited/Intermediate
only. Reciprocity waives the technical examination ONLY; the applicant still files an application,
pays the annual fee, supplies official verification of VA licensure, and passes a 25-question NC
laws-and-rules open-book exam. Source: ncbeec.org/virginia, and VA DPOR's tradesman reciprocal
agreements PDF.]`

`[VERIFY — GENERAL CONTRACTING, UNCONFIRMED. NC G.S. § 87-15.1 permits the Board, IN ITS
DISCRETION, to issue a license of equivalent classification to a GC licensed in another state
without written examination. VA separately lists NC→VA examination waivers for Residential Building
Contractor and Commercial Building Contractor. No standing VA↔NC general-contracting agreement was
confirmed, and the two schemes are structurally different (VA A/B/C by value; NC
Limited/Intermediate/Unlimited by project value). CALL 919-845-4240 / nclbgc.org before assuming it
applies. Only matters for NC work at or above $40,000 — below that, no NC license is needed at all.]`

**Do not overstate self-performance.** If plumbing and electrical are performed by licensed
partners rather than your own crews, say so. It is a normal, defensible arrangement — and it is
what your own About page already says. What is not defensible is claiming crews you don't have
and getting caught on the first billed draw.

**You are buying access, not selling.** The first year of sub work is almost never profitable on
paper. The return is a GC relationship that eventually produces repeat work you schedule
yourself. Price accordingly and do not chase volume that breaks your crews.
